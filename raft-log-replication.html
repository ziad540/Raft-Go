Which comes first?

Check the preceding entry first. Delete only if that check passes and an incoming entry actually conflicts. Then update the commit index. Apply committed entries in order.

An index is a position. The entry at that position stores a command and the term in which the leader created it. It may be applied in a later term. In this lab index 0 holds a dummy entry with term 0, so an empty log has a place to start matching.

  1. Check the RPC term. Reject an older term and return your current term. A newer term makes you adopt it, step down, and clear the old vote. A candidate hearing a leader in its own term also steps down, retaining its vote. A non-stale leader’s message resets the election clock even if the next check fails.
  2. Check the prefix. Does the follower have PrevLogIndex, with term PrevLogTerm? If either check fails, reply false. The log and commit index stay as they were. The leader tries an earlier position.
  3. Compare the incoming entries in order. At the same index and term, keep the entry. At the first missing index, append the rest. At the first existing index with a different term, delete from that index to the end and append the remaining incoming entries. Only uncommitted entries can legitimately conflict. Our implementation rejects an attempt to overwrite a committed entry.
  4. Advance the follower’s commit index. Once the request matches, the follower can trust the leader’s commit announcement only through the last index covered by this request. The formula below enforces that bound.
  5. Deliver committed commands. The lab’s applier sends each index from lastApplied + 1 through commitIndex to applyCh. The service executes the command. Raft releases its mutex before the channel send so a slow service can’t stall RPC handling.

Why does checking one preceding entry work? Each term has at most one elected leader, which creates at most one entry per index. Equal index and term identify the same command. Starting from the empty log, every accepted extension attaches to a matching prefix. So equal index and term also establish the same history through that position.

How the leader repairs a log
When elected, the leader sets each follower’s nextIndex to its own last index plus one. That is its first guess at what to send. matchIndex starts at 0 for each follower and records how far that follower has actually confirmed. A heartbeat is an AppendEntries with zero entries. It still checks the prefix and carries LeaderCommit.


In Figure 7, the term-8 leader has entries through index 10. Its first probe for every follower uses nextIndex = 11, so it asks whether index 10 has term 6.

After a successful request, the leader knows this follower matches through PrevLogIndex + len(Entries). If that index is 10, then matchIndex = 10 and the next entry to send is 11. A lost reply proves nothing. Retries are safe because matching entries are skipped. A delayed rejection cannot undo a later confirmed match, and a reply carrying a higher term makes the old leader step down.


When is a command safe to execute?
Start("set x = 10") on a leader stores a proposal and returns its index and term immediately. A follower declines the call. If the leader loses its election term before the proposal reaches a majority, a later leader may replace it. In that case the service never executed it, so there is no change to undo.


The leader counts replicas
For a cluster of five, a majority is three servers, including the leader. The leader finds the highest index N above its commit index for which at least three servers have matchIndex ≥ N and log[N].Term == currentTerm. It can then commit everything through N. It cannot count copies of an older-term entry to declare that entry committed on its own. Figure 8 in §5.4.2 shows how that older entry could still be overwritten after another election.


The follower uses the leader’s announcement
lastCovered = PrevLogIndex + len(Entries)
safeThrough = min(LeaderCommit, lastCovered)
commitIndex = max(commitIndex, safeThrough)


Suppose LeaderCommit = 8, but this successful RPC covers only entries 5 and 6, following index 4. The follower can advance from 3 to 6. Its local log might extend to 9, but this request established a match only through 6. For an empty heartbeat, lastCovered = PrevLogIndex. A delayed announcement can never move commitment backward.


Each peer tracks three positions
lastApplied ≤ commitIndex ≤ last local log index
If those values are 6 ≤ 8 ≤ 10, entries 7 and 8 are ready for delivery. Entries 9 and 10 are stored, but the peer lacks confirmation to apply them. After delivery, lastApplied reaches 8. A repeated heartbeat announcing 8 causes no duplicate execution.


What if the leader changes?
In this lab a follower or candidate that times out starts a new term, votes for itself, and requests votes in parallel. The timeout is randomized between 400 and 800 ms. A leader sends heartbeats about every 100 ms. A candidate needs a majority in its own election term. A split vote leads to another election after timeout. Delayed votes from the previous round do not count.
A voter considers at most one candidate per term, allowing retries from that candidate. It compares last log term first, then last log index if the terms tie. The candidate’s election term and last log term are separate. The voter never compares the two servers’ commit indexes. Majority overlap together with this voting restriction preserves committed history.

If B had been missing entries 7–8 entirely, A would have needed C’s copy to commit them in the first place. In that version of the example, C rejects B’s shorter log with an older last term or the same last term. B alone cannot win.


Check your understanding


A follower has 12 entries. A heartbeat matches at 10. What is deleted?
Nothing. The prefix check succeeds and there are no incoming entries to conflict with 11 or 12. The heartbeat can announce commitment only through 10.


The preceding term is wrong, but LeaderCommit is high. What changes?
Reject the request. The log and commit index stay unchanged. If the leader’s term was current or newer, the follower still resets its election timer.


A term-5 leader finds an entry from term 4 on three of five servers. What can it conclude?
Those copies alone do not allow the new leader to advance commitment. Committing a term-5 entry on a majority also commits the preceding prefix.
